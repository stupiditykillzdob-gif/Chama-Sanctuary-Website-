Wild Raptor Habitat & Visiting Educational Falconry

Wild Raptor Protection

Our 1,917-acre Chama Valley refuge provides critical hunting and nesting territory for Prairie Falcons, Peregrine Falcons, Golden Eagles, Red-tailed Hawks, and Great Horned Owls. We maintain strict 660-foot seasonal nesting buffers, rodenticide-free pastures, and elevated hunting perches across open meadows.

Visiting Educational Workshops

Rather than housing captive birds on-site, The D.G. Chama Sanctuary Project partners with credentialed, non-resident master falconers holding active USFWS and New Mexico Department of Game and Fish (NMDGF) educational permits to host supervised wildlife demonstrations for disabled veterans and youth cohorts.

Frequently Asked Questions

How are disabled veteran retreats funded?

Disabled veteran cohorts are funded through Founding Corporate Sponsorships, individual tax-deductible donations, and veteran adaptive outdoor grants—allowing veterans to attend cabin retreats, guided fishing, and mentored hunts at zero or nominal personal cost.

Are predators hunted or trapped on the sanctuary?

No. Hunting, snaring, or recreational trapping of bears, mountain lions, coyotes, bobcats, or foxes is strictly prohibited across all 1,917 acres. Staff and guests carry EPA-registered bear spray as the primary first line of defense, and any habituated nuisance wildlife is managed exclusively through non-lethal live capture and relocation by the New Mexico Department of Game and Fish (NMDGF).

What fishing and hunting ethics govern the property?

All sanctuary trout waters are barbless, catch-and-release only with sanitized on-site waders to prevent Whirling Disease. Big-game wildlife management hunts are strictly non-motorized, fair-chase, walk-in only, and use 100% lead-free solid copper ammunition.
