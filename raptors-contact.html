Wild Raptor Habitat & Visiting Educational Falconry

Rather than confining permanent resident birds in cages or aviaries, The D.G. Chama Sanctuary Project invests directly in wild rangeland habitat enhancements across our 1,917 acres—supporting native Bald and Golden Eagles, Red-tailed Hawks, Cooper’s Hawks, Peregrine Falcons, Prairie Falcons, American Kestrels, Ospreys, and Great Horned Owls:

  • Wild Raptor Infrastructure: We install 6 to 8 natural cedar and pine hunting perches, kestrel and owl nesting boxes, and high-visibility vinyl fence flight diverters along rangeland fencing, while enforcing year-round closures on our 200-Acre Upland Breeding Escarpment and a 660-foot seasonal buffer around any active Bald or Golden Eagle nests.
  • Lead-Free & Rodenticide-Free Standards: We require 100% lead-free fishing tackle and strictly prohibit chemical rodenticides to protect raptors from secondary poisoning.
  • Visiting Educational Falconry Demonstrations: We partner with visiting credentialed Master Falconers and licensed raptor educators to host permitted glove-handling and flight workshops in an open meadow clearing inside our 100-Acre Sanctuary Core (set at least 200 yards away from our trout ponds, homestead ring, and breeding escarpment).

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

  • Is the sanctuary open to walk-in public traffic? No. All visits, retreats, and sponsor tours operate strictly by pre-scheduled appointment to protect wildlife habitat and guest privacy.
  • Are ATVs or off-road vehicles allowed? No. All commercial and hosted hunting is strictly non-motorized (foot-travel only) outside designated entry roads.
  • Is predator hunting allowed? No. Bears, mountain lions, coyotes, bobcats, and foxes are strictly protected, and all staff and guests carry EPA-registered bear spray in the field.
  • Are smoking or vaping allowed? Strictly prohibited across all sanctuary grounds except inside two isolated, fire-safe designated stations.
