Birds of Prey & Raptor Conservation

Protecting Northern New Mexico’s Native Raptors & High-Country Skies.

Rather than confining permanent resident birds in cages or aviaries, The D.G. Chama Sanctuary Project invests directly in wild rangeland habitat enhancements across our 1,917 acres—supporting native Bald and Golden Eagles, Red-tailed Hawks, Prairie and Peregrine Falcons, Ospreys, and Great Horned Owls.

Wild Raptor Habitat Structures

We install 6 to 8 natural cedar and pine hunting perches, kestrel and owl nesting boxes, and high-visibility vinyl fence flight diverters, while enforcing year-round closures on our 200-Acre Upland Breeding Escarpment and a 660-foot buffer around active eagle nests.

Lead-Free & Rodenticide-Free

We require 100% lead-free fishing tackle and strictly prohibit chemical rodenticides across all sanctuary land to protect raptors from secondary poisoning.

Visiting Educational Falconry

We partner with visiting credentialed Master Falconers and licensed raptor educators to host permitted glove-handling and flight workshops in an open meadow clearing inside our 100-Acre Sanctuary Core (at least 200 yards from trout ponds and breeding zones).

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the sanctuary open to general walk-in public traffic?

No. All retreats, workshops, and sponsor tours operate strictly by pre-scheduled appointment to protect wildlife habitat and guest privacy.

Are ATVs or off-road vehicles allowed during hunts?

No. All commercial and hosted hunting is strictly non-motorized (foot-travel only) outside designated entry roads.

Is predator hunting allowed on the sanctuary?

No. Hunting or trapping bears, mountain lions, coyotes, bobcats, or foxes is strictly prohibited. All staff and guests carry EPA-registered bear spray in the field.

Are smoking or vaping permitted?

Strictly prohibited everywhere on sanctuary grounds except inside two isolated, fire-safe designated stations.
