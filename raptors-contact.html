Birds of Prey & Raptor Conservation

Across the 1,917 acres of The D.G. Chama Sanctuary Project in Chama, New Mexico, native birds of prey—including Bald and Golden Eagles, Red-tailed Hawks, Prairie and Peregrine Falcons, Ospreys, and Great Horned Owls—are essential indicators of a healthy mountain ecosystem.

Wild Raptor Habitat Structures

Rather than confining permanent resident birds in cages or aviaries, we install 6 to 8 natural cedar and pine hunting perches, kestrel and owl nesting boxes, and high-visibility vinyl fence flight diverters—while enforcing a 660-foot seasonal buffer around active eagle nests.

Lead-Free & Rodenticide-Free

All angling strictly requires 100% lead-free tungsten-beaded nymphs, and we never use chemical rodenticides on sanctuary land—protecting native hawks, eagles, falcons, and owls from secondary poisoning.

Visiting Educational Falconry

We partner with visiting credentialed Master Falconers and licensed raptor educators to host permitted, glove-handling and flight workshops in an open meadow arena inside our 100-Acre Sanctuary Core (set at least 200 yards from our trout ponds and breeding escarpment).

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

1. Is the sanctuary open to general walk-in public traffic?

No. To protect wildlife habitat, preserve quiet solitude for disabled veterans and youth cohorts, and maintain strict safety and biosecurity protocols, The D.G. Chama Sanctuary Project operates exclusively by pre-scheduled retreat, workshop, or sponsor appointment.

2. How do disabled veterans apply for a retreat or hosted hunt?

Disabled veterans, military bases, and veteran service organizations can email info@dgchamasanctuaryproject.com directly to request seasonal availability, mobility accommodation details, and program registration forms. All retreats are offered at low cost to eliminate financial barriers.

3. How does the Single-Parent & Youth Outdoor Mentorship Program work?

Our spring and fall youth retreats welcome single-parent families for weekend mentorship in ethical wild turkey hunting and catch-and-release trout fishing. Every minor child stays in the exact same cabin unit with their accompanying parent or legal guardian, and all dock sessions enforce a strict 2:1 youth-to-adult spotter ratio.

4. What are the fishing rules on the sanctuary’s trout ponds?

All fishing on our two solar-fed Rio Grande Cutthroat trout ponds is 100% catch-and-release using barbless, single-hook, lead-free tungsten-beaded nymphs. Anglers wear sanctuary-issued rubber-soled boots sanitized on-site before entering the water.

5. Are ATVs, UTVs, or off-road vehicles allowed during hunts?

No. All commercial and hosted hunting on sanctuary property is strictly non-motorized (foot-travel only) outside designated entry roads.

6. Is predator hunting or smoking allowed on the sanctuary?

Hunting or trapping native predators (bears, mountain lions, coyotes, bobcats, or foxes) is strictly prohibited, and all staff and guests carry EPA-registered bear spray. Smoking and vaping are strictly prohibited everywhere except inside two isolated, fire-safe designated stations.
